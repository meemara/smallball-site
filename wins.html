The wins

The small wins we build most often.

Eight places where a few hours a week are quietly leaking out of most small businesses — and the small, practical fix for each one. None of these is a transformation. That's the point.

Email management

What it looks like: the inbox runs the day. Hours disappear into sorting, replying, and re-reading long threads — and the message that actually mattered is buried under forty that didn't.

The small fix: an AI coworker that triages the inbox, drafts replies in your words for your review, summarizes the long threads, and flags the handful of messages that truly need you.

The inbox stops running the day — you do

Invoice & payment reminders

What it looks like: you did the work, sent the invoice, and now somebody spends part of every week chasing money — awkward calls, "just following up" emails, things slipping.

The small fix: a polite, personal-sounding reminder sequence — day 3, day 7, overdue — that runs itself. Vendor invoices read automatically instead of re-typed.

One contractor: ~6 hours/week back on chasing alone
See it running in my own business →

Scheduling & call prep

What it looks like: back-and-forth to book anything, walking into calls cold, and jobs sitting idle between phases because the next step waits on a phone call.

The small fix: booking that runs itself, a one-page prep doc built automatically before every call, and phase-done triggers that start the next step without you.

Roughly 130 hours/year back; phase gaps from days to hours

Quotes & estimates

What it looks like: 45 minutes of cold drafting per quote — pulling scope, pricing, and boilerplate together from scratch — so quotes go out late, or not at all.

The small fix: AI drafts the first pass from your scope notes, your pricing, and your own past quotes. You review and send.

Cold drafting becomes a 15-minute edit
See it running in my own business →

Lead intake & qualification

What it looks like: a lead comes in Friday at 7pm and gets an answer Monday at 10 — after they've already called your competitor.

The small fix: instant first response any hour, a few qualifying questions, the meeting booked, and the lead logged — judgment calls still yours.

Faster response, better show rates, nothing slips

Expense & receipt capture

What it looks like: a shoebox of receipts, a spreadsheet somebody dreads, and month-end taking days longer than it should.

The small fix: photo the receipt, the data reads itself, the right person approves it. The spreadsheet hour disappears.

Month-end closes days sooner
See it running in my own business →

Weekly client updates

What it looks like: clients calling to ask "where are we at?" because updates only happen when somebody remembers.

The small fix: milestone updates with photos that send themselves on schedule. Clients stop wondering — and consistency like that is what gets you referred.

The follow-through your competitors don't have

Data collection & reports

What it looks like: the numbers you need live in three systems, a few spreadsheets, and somebody's head. Reports get built by hand at month-end — or they don't get built at all, and you run the business on gut feel.

The small fix: the data collects itself into one place, and the reports you actually use build themselves on a schedule — same format, every week, without anyone burning an afternoon on it.

The three rules that make these wins stick

Automate the when, not the judgment

Machines are great at "send this on day 3." They're bad at "decide if this customer deserves a discount." We hand off the schedule and keep the judgment yours.

Fix the process first

Automating a broken process just breaks it faster. We lean the workflow up before we put AI on it — the step most AI consultants skip.

Always track a metric

An automation nobody measures quietly dies in six months. Every win we build comes with a number you can watch.
