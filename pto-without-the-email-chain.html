PTO requests without the email chain.

In most small companies, time-off lives in texts, sticky notes, and one overworked person's memory — and the people scheduling crews are the last to find out.

The problem

A PTO request used to pass through about four people: the ask, the “let me check,” the balance dig, the approval, the calendar entry someone meant to make. Beyond the busywork, the real damage was downstream — project managers would build a crew around someone who, it turned out, had been approved for that week off. Surprise absences wreck a schedule.

What runs

Now it's a pipeline of five small pieces, built one at a time:

  • Employee fills a 30-second form.
  • Their manager gets an approval card with the live balance already on it — and the math of what's left if they say yes.
  • One tap approves it. It's on the team calendar before the employee walks back to their desk. A denial sends a clear answer instead of silence.
  • Balances stay current on their own — an automation reads the Paychex PDF report with OCR and updates the numbers, no one re-keying anything.
  • Monday mornings, project managers get a two-week who's-out digest so they schedule crews around who's actually going to be there.

The numbers

~20 requests a month Four people's back-and-forth, gone PMs see who's out before they build a crew

The honest part

We didn't build this as one system. The form came first and worked alone. Approvals came later. Automatic balances came last. Nobody's workflow blew up on any given Tuesday — and the biggest payoff turned out to be the calendar, not the time saved: schedules stopped getting blindsided.
This runs in my own operation today.
